Week 6: The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804)
Comprehensive Academic Lecture Notes (TR & EN)
1. Sociodemographic Powder Keg & Universal Rights Contradiction
Universal Rights Collision: When the French Revolution declared universal Rights of Man, these principles collided directly with colonial slave societies like San Domingo.
World's Richest Colony: Producing 40% of Europe's sugar and 60% of its coffee, San Domingo's colonial wealth directly funded the French merchant bourgeoisie.
Brutal Demographic Imbalance: 500,000 enslaved Africans worked under 40,000 whites (12-to-1 ratio). Planters used systematic terror to suppress revolts, while fresh African arrivals kept warrior traditions alive.
2. Vodou Networks, Insurrection & Emancipation
Vodou & Maroon Networks: Escaped slaves formed Maroon communities. Leaders like Mackandal and Dutty Boukman used Vodou rituals (Bois Caïman 1791) to organize a massive, coordinated slave uprising.
Toussaint Louverture's Leadership: Toussaint built a disciplined army, defeating British and Spanish invaders. Facing white royalist treason, French Commissioner Sonthonax abolished slavery in 1793, legalized by the Paris Convention in 1794.
Toussaint's 1801 Constitution: Toussaint created an autonomous state with its own constitution and army, naming himself Governor-for-Life under nominal French ties.
3. Napoleon's Betrayal, War of Independence & 1804 Republic
Napoleon's Leclerc Expedition: Napoleon sent 20,000 troops in 1801 to crush Toussaint and restore slavery. Toussaint was betrayed and shipped to die in a cold French prison (1803).
Total Anti-Colonial War: Napoleon's reinstatement of slavery in Guadeloupe ignited total anti-colonial war. General Jean-Jacques Dessalines led relentless guerrilla warfare, decimating French forces.
Proclamation of Haiti (1804): On January 1, 1804, independence was proclaimed under the indigenous Taíno name *Haiti*, creating the first free black republic and the only successful slave revolt in human history.
