Week 5: The French Revolution & Birth of the Nation-State
Comprehensive Academic Lecture Notes (TR & EN)
1. Ancien Régime Breakdown & Enlightenment Critique
Ancien Régime Injustice: Absolute France divided society into Three Estates: Clergy (1st), Nobility (2nd), and the Third Estate (98% of population, carrying the entire tax burden).
Rise of Bourgeoisie: Wealth from colonial sugar/slave trade enriched French merchants, but Ancien Régime laws blocked them from political power, fueling bourgeois revolutionary ambition.
Desacralizing Monarchy: Salons and underground pamphlets (*libelles*) exposed royal corruption, stripping monarchy of sacred divine right long before barricades were built.
2. Fiscal Collapse & Birth of National Assembly
Sovereign Bankruptcy: Funding the American Revolution pushed France into near-bankruptcy, forcing Louis XVI to convoke the Estates-General in May 1789.
Tennis Court Oath & Bastille: The Third Estate declared itself the National Assembly on June 17. On July 14, Parisian crowds stormed the Bastille, turning a constitutional crisis into a popular revolution.
Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789): Proclaimed liberty, equality, and national sovereignty. Article 17 declared property an inviolable right, replacing noble birth with wealth and talent.
3. Constitutional Monarchy, Radicalization & Republic
Class Exclusions: The 1791 Constitution restricted voting to property-owning 'active citizens', disenfranchising workers and igniting radical *sans-culotte* protests.
War & Fall of Monarchy: Louis XVI's flight to Varennes and war against Austria/Prussia destroyed faith in monarchy. Volunteer armies halted invaders at Valmy (1792).
Proclamation of Republic: The National Convention declared France a Republic with universal male suffrage and executed Louis XVI in January 1793.
