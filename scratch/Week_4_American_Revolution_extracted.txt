Week 4: The American Revolution & Constitutional Settlement
Comprehensive Academic Lecture Notes (TR & EN)
1. Transatlantic Intellectual Foundations & Colonial Typology
Age of Revolutions: The American Revolution transformed loyal British colonists into revolutionaries, pioneering modern constitutional republicanism based on consent of the governed.
Colonial Typology & Land Ownership: Lacking a rigid feudal aristocracy, broad frontier land availability allowed 60% to 80% of white adult males to own land and vote, fostering an independent middle class.
Salutary Neglect: For over a century, Britain exercised minimal direct interference, allowing colonial assemblies to act as de facto sovereign parliaments controlling local taxation.
2. Crisis of Imperial Taxation & Escalation
Seven Years' War Fiscal Crisis: Victory over France in 1763 doubled British national debt, prompting Parliament to end salutary neglect and levy direct taxes (Stamp Act 1765).
Lockean Property Rights: Colonists rallied around 'No Taxation Without Representation', arguing unconsented taxation violated Lockean natural property rights.
Escalation & Outbreak: Coercive Acts following the 1773 Boston Tea Party closed Boston harbor and dissolved self-government. Armed skirmishes at Lexington & Concord (1775) launched revolutionary war.
3. Declaration of Independence & Constitutional Compromise
Declaration of Independence (1776): Thomas Jefferson codified Lockean natural rights—life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness—declaring government legitimacy derives solely from the consent of the governed.
French Alliance & Yorktown: French naval blockades and military forces under Lafayette secured decisive victory at Yorktown (1781).
1787 Constitutional Settlement: The Constitutional Convention established a federal republic with checks & balances. The Great Compromise created a bicameral Congress, while shelving slavery preserved immediate union but planted the seeds of the Civil War.
