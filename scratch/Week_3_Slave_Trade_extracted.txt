Week 3: The Transatlantic Slave Trade (1519–1867)
Comprehensive Academic Lecture Notes (TR & EN)
1. Scale, Scope & Uniqueness of Transatlantic Slavery
Massive Forced Migration: Between 1519 and 1867, over 12.5 million captive Africans were shipped across the Atlantic. The expansion of plantation agriculture—sugar, tobacco, coffee—demanded massive labor forces.
Unique Chattel Nature: Transatlantic slavery was unique in human history: unlike ancient Roman or Ottoman servitude, it turned human beings into permanent, hereditary chattel property based strictly on racial demarcation.
Capital Accumulation: Profits from colonial trade fleets, silver mining, and plantation agriculture enriched European merchant cartels and banking networks, laying the financial groundwork for the Industrial Revolution.
2. Atlantic Prototypes, Sugarcane & African Expertise
Atlantic Island Prototypes: In the 15th century, Portuguese and Spanish navigators conquered Atlantic islands (Madeira, São Tomé) to test sugarcane monoculture powered by forced African labor.
Native Collapse & Legal Protection Paradox: Native demographic collapse and Crown regulations (Valladolid Controversy 1550) shifted colonial labor demand onto unprotected African captives.
West African Pastoral Expertise: Captives imported from Wolof, Fula, and Mandinga brought vital cattle-herding, blacksmithing, and agricultural skills that built early American ranches and plantations.
3. Commercial Mechanisms, Gun-Slave Cycle & Race Construction
Coastal Forts & Sovereign Parity: 64 European coastal forts (Elmina, Luanda) conducted trade with sovereign African states, exchanging textiles, metals, cowrie shells, and firearms for captives.
Gun-Slave Cycle: European firearms introduced a security dilemma among African kingdoms, forcing states to acquire guns by trading war captives to defend against armed neighbors.
Virginia Case Study & Race Construction: In early 17th-century Virginia, social status was defined by property, not skin color. Following Bacon's Rebellion (1676), planter elites enacted racial slave codes to fracture working-class unity and guarantee permanent plantation labor.
