Week 11
Egypt’s Path to Modernization, 1801-1882
In pursuing our line of inquiry in this course we have pretty much remained focused on the West
In this context, modernization= Westernization
But is this necessarily always the case?
SLIDE
Throughout the 19th c. a number of non-Western societies will initiate a period of modernization of their institutions, their armies and their technology while at the same time preserving their cultural distinctiveness
Meiji Japan, Ottoman Empire after Mahmut II
The most emblematic case, because one of the first ones, was Egypt, an Ottoman Province which for a long while followed its own trajectory
SLIDE
Muhammad Ali (Mehmet Ali)=> ethnic Albanian born and raised in the Greek city of Kavalla
Arrives in Egypt in 1801 as part of the joint Ottoman-British force that routed the French
One of the leaders of the Albanian contingent
After the French left, power vacuum=> Muhammad Ali quickly imposes himself on the other warlords
Recognized by the Ottoman Sultan as governor of Egypt in 1805
MA along with other Ottoman officers impressed by the engineering of the French and the military might of European armies
SLIDE
His whole reign is marked by a drive towards modernization in all sections of the administration and society + important efforts towards state-building
Europeanization and modernization of the armed forces
Creation of a centralized bureaucracy
Transformation of the agricultural landholding pattern and agricultural production
Creates a big Egyptian empire at the expense of the Ottoman Empire which by the 1830s includes
Sudan
Western coast of Arabia (Hijaz)
Greater Syria
Parts of southwest Anatolia
The result of this activity is twofold:
Egypt is transformed from a subaltern Ottoman province into a full-fledged state
During all these policies Egypt is brought in constant contact with the West and European powers
In the long-term Muhammad Ali’s objective was to secure the independence of Egypt and establish a dynastic form of rule on that newly independent country
This is why the main focus of his reforms bears on the reform of the army
SLIDE
First step for this is to destroy the Mamluks
In 1811 he invites 74 Mamluk leaders to a banquet and massacres them in the Cairo Citadel
Second step is to open an officer’s training school in Aswan with European instructors and Turkish and Arabic students
SLIDE
[Fahmy] The influence of this newly created military elite would be felt way beyond the strict confines of the army=> a more global impact on society and the state
Creation of a series of educational institutions initially meant to support the army
Schools of medicine, veterinary medicine, engineering and chemistry
All the education system is turned towards the West=> high need of proficiency in languages
1835 opening of the School of Languages for the purpose of training translators and preparing Arabic textbooks
This school=> profound influence on the direction of Egypt’s cultural and educational life until its closure in the 1850s
Also need for a printing office to print and publish all of the government’s orders and policies and the government’s official gazette
Embrace of the print is a clear break with Ottoman policy
+ Facilitates spread of Westernization
SLIDE
Army then is the spearhead of the modernization program but where to get such an army?
At first Muhammad Ali thinks about creating a slave-army of Sudanese
Hence invasion of the Sudan in 1820
More successfully=> creation of an army of conscripts made up by the Egyptian fellahin themselves
Creation of a peasant-army that in 1830 numbered 130,000
Conscription itself is a new form of government because to have a successful conscription you need:
Need for accurate statistics=> Census that can be used for tax collection as well
One of the long-term consequences of conscription is the rise of nationalism among mainly Arabic soldiers
SLIDE
But where do you get the money for a such a big and powerful army?
Reform of the tax collection system +
Taxes imposed on the Waqf (Evkaf)
Imposition of the Waqf also diminishes power of the ulama (ulema)
Experimentation also with new crops
Long-staple cotton known as Jumel
This Jumel cotton is the favorite of the European textile industry and therefore becomes Egypt’s main cash crop
SLIDE
Cultivation and agricultural reform also means bringing more lands under cultivation
Rebuilding and expanding the irrigation system
Thousands of fellahin are used as forced labor to build and dredge canals and construct barrages so that the annual Nile flood can be stored for the long dry summer
Extensive use of the corvée or forced labor system
Cf. digging and dredging of the Mahmudiyyah Canal=> Alexandria to Mediterranean Sea and Nile
In order to introduce diversity in Egypt’s economy, Muhammad Ali introduces a program of industrialization based on the production of war-related material (textile and guns)
Machinery and managers are imported from Europe
But the workers and the craftsmen are Egyptian fellahin
SLIDE
The whole industrialization policy is driven through a monopoly system
M.A. forces cultivators to sell to him directly at a fixed price
Then he sells to Europeans to the higher market price
This the Europeans do not like=> They cannot buy directly from the cultivators and are forced to buy from the governor at a price decided by him
SLIDE
Because there is a monopoly system driven by the state, you need a modern centralized state
Centralization of the administration= all powers taken from former elites
Creation of specialized ministries
Appearance of a group of intermediary professional government officials trained in specialized administrative and technological schools and appointed on the basis of their qualifications
However MA still continues to run this centralized government as a household appointing his sons and relatives to key positions
SLIDE
Many have interpreted this new era for Egypt as the awakening of an Egyptian nation-state
However MA himself is a dynast and does not identify at all with Egyptians who are just a means to the end of his own power
The language of the administration continues to be Ottoman Turkish
The initial composition of the government and military elite is essentially Turks and Circassians
Also relies a lot on minorities (Christians)
His most trusted adviser, Boghos Pasha is an Armenian
Gradually however, through the schools that he opened and the army, emergence of an Arabic Egyptian elite
One generation after MA dies, Arabic replaced Ottoman Turkish as the language of the administration
SLIDE
MA’s reign is also known for his extensive military campaigns throughout the Middle East
At first the wars are fought for the Ottoman Sultan
His son Ibrahim Pasha
War against the Wahhabis who had captured the Holy Cities of Mecca and Medina
Conquest of the Sudan begins in the early 1820s against the Blue Sultanate of the Fenj
Almost overturned the outcome of the Greek War of Independence in 1824-1827 before his fleet is destroyed in Navarino, 1827 by the French, British and Russians
Gradually however, they become personal wars to carve himself an Empire from within the Ottoman Empire
Begins with the conquest of Greater Syria in 1831
During this campaign Ibrahim actually pushed almost to Kütahya, 150 miles from Istanbul
During the campaign Ottoman Empire and the Russians sign the Treaty of Hünkar İskelesi to stop Egyptian progression 1833
Perceived as deadly danger by the British because appears to facilitate Russian access to the Mediterranean
French and  British press MA and the Sultan to stop fighting
Signing of a Treaty whereby Ibrahim becomes the governor of Adana and of all Greater Syria
Ibrahim introduces many of his father’s Egyptian reforms to Syria
SLIDE
Unrest in Syria convinces the Ottoman Sultan to organize a campaign against Ibrahim in 1839
Sultan’s forces are defeated at the Battle of Nezib=> Another international diplomatic crisis
Britain sends fleet in Beirut in a joint British-Ottoman operation against MA in 1840
Ibrahim is forced to retreat to Egypt
MA forced to sign a Treaty in 1841:
He can only keep Sudan
He has to reduce his army to 18,000 troops
HOWEVER, the Treaty also recognizes the Egyptian government as a hereditary dynasty=> MA’s descendants will rule Egypt until 1952.
SLIDE
Egypt after MA would be in considerably weaker position
Already in 1838 the British had imposed on the Ottoman Sultan the Treaty of Baltalimanı which provided for the abolition of monopolies throughout the Empire
This includes Egypt and therefore destroys the foundation of Egypt’s path to industrialization
In addition, the Treaty of 1841, by forcibly reducing MA’s army=> weakening of all the industries related to army and military
Many of the schools meant to staff this army are closed as well
It can be argued that one long-term effect of MA’s economic policies and military adventures is to have involved the Western powers deeply into the affairs of the Middle East
We saw, then the policies implemented by MA to modernize Egypt + make it an independent state
Although Egypt changed considerably during MA’s reign (army-led modernization)
Problem with the war and the limitation of Egypt’s initiative in modernization
1838 Treaty of Balta Liman and end of the Monopoly System
1841 Treaty of London and limitation of Egypt’s army
However when MA passed away in 1849, his successors continue his policies of modernization
This is then today’s topic
SLIDE
The successors of MA (Abbas and Sa’id) are not as consistent or skillful as him, however the administrative reforms continue
Centralization continues
This feeds an increase of the administrative elite
SLIDE
Cf. career of engineer and military instructor Ali Mubarak (1823-1893) extremely influential in the modernization of Egypt and the first native Egyptian to become a Minister
Career also of Rifa’a al-Tahtawi (1801-1873) who exercised a big influence on Egypt’s cultural life
After spending five years in Paris, this imam formed at the Al-Azhar mosque was actually the one to persuade MA to found the School of Languages
SLIDE
Meanwhile, end of the monopoly system and the army-led industrialization transforms Egyptian economy
It is now very much shaped by the needs of the European market.
Egyptian economy becomes a virtual plantation economy, exporting raw materials (cotton) and importing European manufactured goods
Nonetheless this cash crop economy still has modernization effects
To export the cotton you need to transport at the lesser cost to the ports
Development of the railway system and in 1852 the first rail link in Egypt is created between Cairo and Alexandria
Also, better integration of Egypt in the whole Mediterranean market
Steamship lines begin to link Alexandria to several Mediterranean ports
This connection of Egypt to the broader Mediterranean and then global market is what ultimately would seal Egypt’s fate
SLIDE
All these new projects + the end of the state monopoly + the enforcement of the Capitulations=> arrival of many Europeans to Egypt between 1850s and 1860s
All interested in transport construction, cotton exchange, concession hunting
By 1872 there were 800,000 Europeans in Egypt over half of whom Greeks and Italians
Under the Capitulations system these people are exempted from taxes and receive the protection of their consuls
Foreigners are almost a state within the state
Foreigners are even appointed to government positions including given ministries
SLIDE
This tendency is greatly increased under Isma’il, MA’s grandson who wants nothing less than the complete Europeanization of Egypt
“My country is no longer in Africa, it is now in Europe.”
Main focus of Isma’il is the education (and its Europeanization)=> increase of the education budget
Dar al-Ulum (founded 1872)= school meant to retrain religious schools to become teachers of Arabic in the national primary and secondary education system
In 1868 the School of Languages is reopened and by 1886 it becomes the Cairo School of Law
French-based legal education preparing future high civil servants and state employees
But the regime does not change at all: authoritarian monarchy, clearly separated from the people
In addition, like MA, he seeks independence from OE
But the way he tries that is through diplomacy, not warfare and by bribing the Sublime Porte
Obtains in 1863 to be recognized as “khedive” a Persian term meaning lord or master
+ right to issue own currency.
SLIDE
One important legal change during Isma’il’s rule is the introduction of Mixed Courts in 1876 to deal with civil and commercial cases
Increase in the number of foreigners and therefore of disputes with the Egyptians
This is an important transformation of the Capitulations system which was abusive to Egyptians and overprotecting of the foreigners (judged by their own consuls based on the laws of their country of origin)
The Mixed Courts deal with affairs disputes between Egyptians and foreigners
For disputes among Egyptians=> National Courts
The problem is that both (Mixed and National) Courts are staffed essentially with European judges
SLIDE
Isma’il is so concerned with transforming his country into a European country that he spends huge sums:
First to transform the cities themselves
Sections of Cairo transformed to look like Paris with boulevards, parks, waterworks, gaslights and tramways
Alexandria=> bridges, railways etc.
Crowning achievement in the field of transportation is the completion of the Suez Canal in 1869
SLIDE
Ideas for the canal were ancient (3rd century BC, Ptolemies), became serious obsession among certain French utopist engineers trained at Polytechnic or Central schools (Saint-simonianism=> maximizing but also channeling forces of industrialization)
Michel Chevalier: Physical meeting of East and West=> creation of a new world order in which the male principle, embodied in the rationally minded West, would enter unto union with the female principle, embodied in the mysterious life forces of the east: “to make the Mediterranean the nuptial bed for a marriage between East and West and to consummate the marriage by the piercing of a canal through the isthmus of Suez”
These first plans are rejected by Muhammad Ali=> considers them too expensive and little profitable
A canal would divert trade from the Egyptian heartlands, cutting benefits to Alexandria and Cairo
While bringing plenty of profit to Western European businessmen attempting to trade between France or England and India
[SLIDE LESSEPS]
The Frenchman who will carry the conviction of both European partners and Egyptian rulers= Ferdinand de Lesseps, a diplomat, linked to the French Empress Eugenie
His major asset= personal friendship with Sa’id Pasha, viceroy of Egypt (wali) [1854-1863]
Gives Lesseps a firman granting him exclusive power to create a share company for digging the Suez canal and exploiting the canal between the two seas. [99 years from the opening of the canal onwards]=> after that control reverts to Egypt]
Sa’id ends up buying most of the shares
In exchange: The new port built at the end of the Canal is called Port Said
Works begin 1858 and project advances rapidly but creates unease because based on the corvee system: Forced labor:
Thousands of fellaheen are recruited and, from 1862 onwards, simply forced to work by the viceroy
They work with their bare hands
This changes with Sa’id successor, his nephew Ismail Pasha
[SLIDE ISMAIL and DIGGING MACHINES]
Ismail dislikes the corvee system because he is a landowner, close to landowners’ interests and disapproves of a scheme which takes so many fellahin away from the fields
Instead favors mechanization of drilling and therefore which obviously necessitates more funding:
Borel, Lavalley and Company
SLIDE
Lavish festivities in the newly built opera after the Canal is finished in 1869
For this occasion, Isma’il even commissions Giuseppe Verdi to write an opera (Aida) which is played during the ceremonies in a newly opened opera
[SLIDE WITH MARITIME ROUTES]
According to The Times, De Lesseps had converted Africa into an island
Meanwhile, Suez Canal Company acts like autonomous government in the region surrounding the Canal
The Canal becomes a major international waterway reducing the distance and therefore the transportation costs between Europe and the East
The one nation most benefiting from the Canal is Britain with its imperial possessions in India and the Far East
The Canal cuts the distance between London and Bombay in half
Initially the British were cool towards the project but then British business picked up the interest quickly
Feeble financial results for the Company, French-Prussian War=> Ismail left to his own, his debts increase dramatically. Raises another loan in 1872
Solution= sell his shares of the Suez Canal Company
This time the British are quicker to react and beat the French in the auctioning off:
By 1875, Britain controls 44% of all canal shares, biggest shareholder
Disraeli writes to Queen Victoria: ‘It is just settled. You have it Madam.’
By 1881, 80% of the traffic through the Canal is British
SLIDE
How does Isma’il pay for all of this=> Cotton
Benefits from the Civil War in the United States (1861-1865)
British textile industry has to turn almost exclusively to Egyptian cotton
Income from cotton exports rise from 900,000 sterlings to over 10,000,000 in the late 1860s
Isma’il privatizes arable land
He also keeps for himself 1/5th of all the cultivated area
Still however the expenses overall far exceeded the revenues and Isma’il has to contract numerous loans with European banks and bondholders
SLIDE
More debt, more sources of income needed
In 1875 Egypt sells its 44% interest in the Suez Canal Company to the British government
But more debts are contracted
New loans only serve to pay the interest rate on older loans
By 1876 Egypt is in a state of virtual bankruptcy=> European financial institutions pressure their governments to take action to force Egypt to pay its debts
In 1876, establishment in Egypt of a Public Debt Commission headed by European specialists
Absolute control on Egypt’s finances and debts
Isma’il tries to resist
Europeans pressure the Ottoman sultan to end his reign
In 1879, Abdülhamit II deposes Imsa’il and replaces him with his son Tawfiq
SLIDE
Under Tawfiq, payment of the debt is given priority over all other expenses
Amounts to 60% of Egypt’s annual revenue
Egyptians restless against this foreign interference
SLIDE
Among them Ahmed Urabi=> Egyptian of peasant origin who became a colonel in the Egyptian army
Becomes the symbol of resistance of the impoverished Arab peasants against the tax-exempted foreigners and the local lords (effendiyah)
Initiates an armed resistance that has two goals= ending foreign interference and limit the authority of the khedive seen as a puppet of the foreigners
SLIDE
Britain and France very worried about the consequences of the Urabi revolt on two levels
What will happen to the repayment of Egypt’s debts
What kind of threat on the Suez Canal does the Urabi revolt represent?
Seizing the pretext of anti-foreign rioting in Alexandria in June 1882, the British invade Egypt
Egypt had indeed become part of Europe but not as Isma’il had intended.
Urabi is captured, and sentenced to exile for life in Ceylon
SLIDE
Egypt was now a British protectorate with Tawfiq accountable to the diplomatic agent and general consul of Great-Britain who for 24 years, 1883-1907 will be Sir Evelyn Baring Lord Cromer
The interest of Britain for Egypt should be relocated into the wider regional context:
Russian-Turkish rivalry and war 1877-1878=> Britain worried about Russian threats on Route to India
Congress of Berlin revising Treaty of San Stefano and containing Russian gains
On the side, Britain acquires Cyprus
SLIDE
Britain truly the one power that develops a global, world policy: controls key points in world navigation: Gibraltar, Malta, Egypt, Cyprus, Suez and beyond, Aden
